# Life Plan v2: integrated, rich, and honest about what the outside world offers

Status: proposed, replaces the direction of v1. Decisions D1 to D4 block the phases they name.
Date: 2026-09-03
Supersedes: `2026-09-03-life-plan.md` (kept as the record of what was built and why)

## Why a v2

v1 optimised for privacy and minimum: files instead of OAuth, regexes before a
model, text instead of pictures, a list instead of a timeline. It was internally
consistent and it was not the product Edd asked for. The ask, restated in his
words and taken at face value:

1. **+18 means +18.** Once somebody has affirmed it, photographs and real
   descriptions, and a way to search positions by what the person wants.
2. **Calendars means integration.** Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, working with
   that data live. Not a file somebody exports by hand.
3. **A model that enriches the day.** MiniMax or DeepSeek reading the day and
   suggesting what to do with it, including events near where the person lives.
4. **The design has to be professional.** The current `/day` is a form with a
   list on top.

This document does not argue with any of that. It says what each one costs and
where the outside world refuses, so the decisions are made on facts.

### The one that changes the business, said plainly

**Photographs of real people in sexual positions are "adult content" under
Stripe's restricted-business terms.** Tasteful illustrations with plain
descriptions are wellness education and are generally fine; photographs are not,
and the difference is not ours to argue after the fact. If D1 is "photographs",
enForma cannot bill through Stripe at all — not on a separate account, not with
a policy letter — and moves to an adult-friendly processor (CCBill, Segpay,
Epoch), with their fees (typically 10–15% against Stripe's ~3%), their
onboarding, and a separate hosting review for the images themselves. That is a
different company to run, and it is still a real option. It is just not a
detail.

Illustrations sit on the right side of that line and still need Task 0.2's
written confirmation because of the shared account.

## The four decisions

**D1 — What "+18" means, exactly.**
- (a) Commissioned or licensed **illustrations**, explicit in description,
  anatomical in drawing. Stripe-compatible. One-off cost per set.
- (b) **Photographs.** Not Stripe. New processor, new hosting terms, and every
  image is a model-release question.
- (c) **AI-generated illustrations.** Every mainstream image API forbids sexual
  content in its terms; a few allow non-explicit anatomical art on request. Has
  to be checked vendor by vendor before it is a plan. Cheapest if it works.

Recommendation: (a). It is what the competitors did, and it is the only one
that keeps the processor.

**D2 — Which calendars, and who holds the token.**
Google via OAuth (Edd runs the verification), Apple via CalDAV app-specific
password, Microsoft via Graph. All three mean **our server holds a credential
that can read somebody's whole calendar**, encrypted at rest with a key the
server holds — v1 refused exactly this and v2 reverses it. Recommendation:
Google first, the other two behind it on the same `calendar_links` table.

**D3 — Which events source.**
Ticketmaster free to start, PredictHQ when the ticketed subset is not enough.
Both need `users.area` to become a real place (lat/long, from a geocoder — the
browser's own position with permission, or a city typed and geocoded once).
Recommendation: Ticketmaster + browser geolocation.

**D4 — Design direction.**
Whether `/day` keeps the app's "Instrument" theme (dark chalk, cobalt signal,
Geist) with the structure and motion from the loaded skill applied on top, or
whether Pro surfaces get their own look. Recommendation: keep the palette, take
the structure — one product, not two. The skill's white-on-`#f9fafb` bento is
its default, not a rule, and the app's own `DESIGN.md` wins on colour.

## Revised phases

Everything below reuses the v1 engine where it still holds: the entitlement
(`pro_until`, admin rule, grace window), `buildDay`'s placement arithmetic, the
`.ics` parser (it becomes the CalDAV/Google normaliser rather than the front
door), the intake parser as the offline fallback, the gym-events rule.

### V2.1 — `/day` redesigned (built; needs D4; nothing external)

The screen is the **day**, not forms. Applying the loaded skill at variance 8,
motion 6, density 4, as built:

- **A real timeline.** `day-timeline.tsx`: vertical, hours as a ruler at
  64px/hour, every block a span whose height is its duration, two things at
  once side by side (`lanesFor`). Free time is visible *space*; only gaps tall
  enough to carry a label get one. The v1 list read as a spreadsheet and the
  annotation circled exactly that.
- **Now.** A hairline across the track on the current minute with a breathing
  dot (the one perpetual animation, memoised and isolated), and one hero
  figure beside the day: an aurora tile with how long until the next thing
  changes (`nowState`, `day-now-tile.tsx`). Green while free, orange while in
  something. The plan said a pill at the top; the tile is the house's hero
  material and the screen has room for exactly one of them.
- **Forms leave the main view.** Anchors, waking window, preferences, calendar
  file and the intake link move into a side sheet (`day-sheet.tsx`) at
  `/day?edit`, so the back button closes it. Same components, relocated. The
  main view has one action: "Shape my day".
- **Layout.** `2fr 1fr` on `md:` and up, the figure sticky beside the track;
  a single column on a phone with the figure first.
- **Motion.** Blocks stagger in on load and `layout`-animate when an anchor
  changes; spring physics; reduced-motion skips the entrance and stops the dot.
- **Materiality.** Inset 1px rings, the tinted tile shadow on the session
  block only, the house graphite and chalk (D4, not cobalt). Mono for every
  number.
- **States.** A composed empty day with the ruler already drawn. The skeleton
  for events and the expired-token line belong to V2.4 and V2.2, where the
  network arrives.

`motion` v13 was already installed; nothing new was added. The walk
(`test-day-plan.mjs`) drives every form through the sheet and runs axe with
it open.

### V2.2 — Calendars, live (built for Google; needs D2 and Google's verification)

- `calendar_links`: `owner`, `provider`, `secret`, `account`, `last_synced`,
  unique per owner and provider. **Every API rule is null**, so PocketBase
  serves the collection to nobody, member or admin; only privileged code in
  `calendar.pb.js` reads it. The refresh token is sealed with `CALENDAR_SECRET`
  (AES, 32 bytes) before it is written, and the route refuses to connect
  anybody when that key is missing rather than storing a live token in the
  clear.
- Four routes: `POST …/google/start` answers with the consent URL (a URL, not a
  redirect, so no token has to travel in a query string); `GET …/google/callback`
  is the only unauthenticated one and trusts a **signed, ten-minute state** that
  carries the account id; `GET …/status` answers with an email and a date and
  never anything replayable; `GET …/busy` mints a fresh access token per pull;
  `POST …/disconnect` deletes the row first and then asks Google to revoke.
- **Read-only, on purpose.** `calendar.events.readonly` is what keeps Google's
  verification tractable, and the write-back the plan sketched is dropped: the
  day already exports as an `.ics` file the member opens themselves. Apple
  (CalDAV) and Microsoft (Graph) fit the same table and are not built.
- Events are never stored on the server. They are normalised to busy blocks on
  the way out (`utils/google_calendar.js`, tested from the app's own suite) and
  kept only in the member's encrypted local profile. A pull **mirrors**:
  `syncCalendarBusy` replaces every `google` block and leaves a file import
  alone, so a meeting moved in Google moves here and one deleted there
  disappears. Disconnecting takes its blocks with it.
- What does not block time never becomes a block: an all-day birthday, an hour
  the calendar marks free, a cancelled event, and one this person declined.
  Titles are off unless asked for, the same rule the file import already had.
- The walk drives the whole round trip against a fake Google on loopback:
  consent, callback, the sealed token, a pull, titles on, a member refused the
  collection, disconnect and revoke.
- **Not done by code:** Google verification (consent screen, domain proof,
  privacy policy, video). Until then the connection works for up to 100 users
  behind an unverified-app warning.

### V2.3 — The model reads the day (built; DeepSeek is env)

- **No new route.** The plan sketched `/api/enforma/day/enrich`; what shipped
  is the coach proxy that already existed, `/api/minimax/chat/completions`,
  which holds the key, meters every call and caps the account at twenty a
  day. A second route would have repeated all three to add nothing. The client
  side is `lib/day-read.ts`: the prompt, the closed-shape gate and a
  per-session cache keyed by the day's signature.
- **Input:** the day's blocks with what kind of thing each is, the free gaps by
  the clock (`freeGaps`, the same function the timeline draws from), the
  waking window, the stated preferences and what did not fit. Events and
  weather join when V2.4 exists.
- **Output, gated:** a reading of at most two sentences and one suggestion per
  free gap. A suggestion for a gap the day does not have is dropped, a second
  one for the same gap is dropped, dashes become commas, lengths are capped,
  and an answer with no reading is no answer (`validateRead`, tested).
- **Asked for, never automatic.** The day carries the labels somebody typed and
  a call is metered, so the read is a button in the aside, "Read my day", with
  the same where-it-goes sentence the intake uses above it. The suggestions
  are drawn inside the gaps they are about; the reading sits beside the tile.
  Busy is a skeleton, and a failure names which of cap, unreachable or
  unreadable it was. A moved anchor makes the reading stale and it goes.
- `COACH_API_KEY`, `COACH_BASE_URL=https://api.deepseek.com/v1`,
  `COACH_MODEL=deepseek-chat` on the sync server; documented in
  `.env.example` and the README. The proxy substitutes the model and hides
  the key.
- The walk boots its sandbox with a fake coach on loopback and watches the
  gate drop an invented gap, the meter count one call, a reload cost nothing
  and a changed day discard the reading.

### V2.4 — Events near you (built; needs D3)

- **The location stays on the device.** The plan said `users.area` gains
  lat/lng; nothing was added to the server. The browser rounds its position to
  a five-character geohash (a cell about 4.9 km a side, `lib/geohash.ts`) and
  that cell, or a city typed by hand, is what travels and what the profile
  remembers (`LifeProfile.place`). The server and the vendor never see a
  coordinate, and the walk reads the vendor's own log to check.
- `/api/enforma/events/near?geo=` or `?city=` (`events.pb.js`): signed in and
  Pro or admin, refused before the key is looked at; Ticketmaster Discovery,
  `geoPoint` + 25 km or `city`, the next 14 days, twenty at most; normalised
  server-side to our shape (`utils/events.js`, tested from the app's own
  suite); cached in `shared_cache` per cell or city for six hours.
  `TICKETMASTER_BASE_URL` lets a walk point it at a fake.
- **The strip** under the day (`nearby-events.tsx`): an idle state that says
  what leaves the device and offers "Use my location" or a city, a skeleton,
  then cards in a sideways row with when, what, where, a segment tag, "Add to
  my day" and a ticket link (https only). Empty and failed states in plain
  words. Ticketed only, and the hint says so rather than letting an empty strip
  imply an empty town.
- **A tap is an outing** (`LifeProfile.outings`: two hours from the start,
  clipped at midnight, bounded to fifty, past ones pruned). `useDayPlan` reads
  outings as commitments beside the gym RSVPs, so the session moves around
  them; the timeline draws them as events that open the tickets; the card then
  says "On your day" and offers to take it off.
- **Fed to V2.3:** what is on today goes into the reading's prompt as context
  it may suggest for a gap it fits, never invent.
- `culture` is marked built in `member-plan.ts`; its entry says what it still
  is not. PredictHQ stays behind the same route as a key and a mapping.

### V2.5 — Intimate activity, the library and the empty frame (built; art pending D1)

Built to the edge of the illustrations, which is where the money starts.

- **The library is twenty entries**, up from eight, each written rather than
  generated. Eight covered the range of *effort*; twenty is what it takes for
  every filter to be worth pressing, at **five entries minimum behind each
  posture and each effort band** — a filter that answers with one card looks
  broken. Two new fields carry it, `postures` (a plural, because most
  arrangements put the two people in different ones) and `facing`. The floor is
  a test, so the next entry cannot quietly drop a posture below it.
- **Search by intent** (`lib/intimacy-search.ts`, tested): free text over the
  name, description and the practical note, plus effort, posture, facing and
  the limitations filter that already existed. Within an axis the values are
  alternatives; across axes they are requirements. Limitations stay
  subtractive: naming a bad back removes what is unkind to one rather than
  keeping only what advertises. Results are ordered by what explicitly suits
  the limitation named. `activitiesFor` is now one line over it, so there is
  one filter in the module rather than two.
- **The place for the image, and no image.** Every entry carries an `art` field
  holding `{ file, alt, credit, licence }` and every one of them is `null`.
  `components/intimacy-art.tsx` draws the frame at 4:3 either way: the
  illustration when there is one, a dashed empty frame when there is not, with
  one line above the list saying so. An empty frame rather than nothing,
  because collapsing it would make the list reflow the day art arrives and
  would mean reviewing a design whose main visual element is imaginary. No
  stand-in artwork, ever.
- **The brief exists**: `docs/intimacy-illustrations.md` — twenty drawings,
  flat vector, 1200x900 WebP named `<id>.webp` in `public/intimacy/`, what has
  to be visible in each, the licence to ask for, and why photographs are not an
  option while this bills through Stripe. `ATTRIBUTION.md` has the section
  reserved. Adding the first illustration is a file plus four lines.
- **Still not built, still on purpose:** no log. The DPIA governs recording
  anybody's sexual activity and nothing in this phase needs one. No streaks, no
  counts, no calorie figure.
- **D1 is now a purchasing question rather than a design one.** The three
  routes to price are in the brief: one illustrator on a fixed fee, a
  generative model corrected by an illustrator (every mainstream image API
  forbids sexual content in its terms, so this has to be confirmed in writing
  vendor by vendor first), or licensing an existing wellness set. If D1 ever
  becomes photographs, the processor moves and the brief says what that costs.
