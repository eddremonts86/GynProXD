# Life Plan: a day worth following, and what it costs to build

Status: Phase 1 built except the card, on `feat/member-pro`. Everything else proposed.
Date: 2026-09-03

**Phase 1 landed without Stripe, on purpose.** The entitlement axis is real and
proved: `users.pro_until`, a request-hook guard, `/api/enforma/me`, a client
cache with a grace window, a subscription panel, `grant-pro.mjs`, and two audits
(`pro-boundary`, 28 checks, in the required `rules` group; `test-pro`, 20 checks,
in `screens`). Pro can be granted, revoked, extended and read offline today. The
only thing missing is a way to pay for it, which is now Phase 7 and last.

That ordering was Edd's call and it is the right one. An account can be Pro
without a payment provider existing, so Phase 2 is unblocked by a script rather
than by a card; and nothing about Stripe can be verified end to end until there
is both a Stripe account and something to sell. The fleet has the evidence for
what the alternative looks like: HunterReady's payment block is "built, verify
open (needs keys)" and builderhunt's launch register has every KYC, CVR and VAT
item still pending. A third unverifiable integration would be the mechanism
pretending to be a fact that `1757900000_gym_plan.js` already warns about.

**Goal:** a paid member surface where somebody describes the day they have and
the day they want, and enForma arranges what it already knows — the training
day, the plate, the challenge — around the hours they actually control. A
companion asks the questions the fields cannot, a calendar tells it when they
are busy, and an optional adults-only module treats intimacy as an activity
rather than a joke.

**Architecture:** one new singleton record inside the encrypted profile
(`lifeProfile`), one per-day record (`dayPlans`), a scheduler that is pure
arithmetic over anchors, and an intake that reads from a fixed question bank
rather than letting a model invent questions. Payment is a new axis on the
account (`users.pro_until`), enforced on the server at the one route that costs
money. Calendars arrive and leave as `.ics`, because every calendar speaks it
and none of them need an OAuth consent screen.

**Tech stack:** unchanged. Vite 8 + React 19 + TanStack Router + Zustand +
Tailwind v4, PocketBase 0.40 JSVM hooks, Vitest, Node built-ins. No new npm
dependency in any phase.

---

## What this is not

It is not a second planner. `/planner` owns the training week and keeps owning
it; this reads that week and never writes to it. It is not a calendar client —
enForma will not become the place somebody accepts a meeting invitation. And it
is not a nutrition tracker: `nutrition-target.ts` already computes the numbers
and `day-plates.ts` already picks the plate, so the day plan places them and
computes nothing new about food.

The one sentence that decides every argument below: **the scheduler arranges,
the model only reads.** Anything a language model produces here is a question
id, a slot ordering, or one short note. It never invents an activity, never
touches a number, and never sees the intimate half of the profile.

## Four honest problems, decided

### 1. There is no way to charge a member today

`gyms.plan` is a text field a human sets after writing an invoice, and
`docs/features/BACKLOG.md` §3 says why that was acceptable: "a subscription
state machine with no payments behind it is a mechanism pretending to be a
fact." Two hundred euros a month from a business, invoiced by hand, is a
process. Fifteen euros a month from a member is not — nobody is writing four
hundred invoices.

So this feature does not begin with a day plan. It begins with the first real
payment path in the product, and everything else waits behind it. That is
Phase 1, it is the least interesting work in this document, and skipping it
means building a paid feature that cannot be paid for.

**Decision: Stripe Checkout + a webhook that sets a date on the account.** Not a
subscription state machine — one field, `users.pro_until`, and a webhook that
pushes it forward. Cancellation is the absence of a renewal. There is no
`status` enum to get out of step with Stripe's own, because the only question
the app ever asks is "is this date in the future".

**The field is built and the card is not.** Until it is, `grant-pro.mjs` writes
the date, which is how a gym gets its plan and rests on the same argument: a
person running a script on purpose beats a mechanism that half works. It also
makes the gate gradable before there is anything to grade, which every screen
behind Pro needs and which `pro-boundary.mjs` depends on.

**This is not the fleet's first payment path, and that changes the work.** Two
sibling products have been here and their reasoning transfers whole:

- `HunterReady/docs/09-decisions.md` (ADR-034) priced merchant-of-record against
  Stripe at exactly the number this product now charges. At EUR 15 a month
  Stripe costs about EUR 0.73 a subscriber and a merchant of record about EUR
  1.25, so the spread is roughly EUR 52 a month at a hundred subscribers and
  inverts in the high hundreds. **Edd chose Stripe there and took the OSS
  obligation personally.** The same choice here needs re-reading, not
  re-litigating. VAT is due in the buyer's country from the first consumer sale
  into the EU, no processor is a merchant of record, and `automatic_tax` gets
  the rate right while the registration and the quarterly return stay Edd's.
- `builderhunt/docs/operations/stripe-webhooks.md` has the receipt shape to
  copy: verify `Stripe-Signature` before parsing anything, because the signature
  IS the authentication and no session is involved; then durably insert one row
  per unique `(livemode, event_id)` **before** returning 2xx, which is the whole
  idempotency mechanism. Its twenty runbooks are worth one read before the hook
  is written.

PocketBase can verify the signature itself: `$security.hs256(text, secret)`
returns the hex HMAC and `$security.equal` compares without leaking timing, so
no Node sidecar is needed. Checked against
`deploy/pocketbase/.local/pb_data_dev/types.d.ts`. The raw body has to come from
`readerToString(e.request.body)` and never from `e.requestInfo().body`, which is
already parsed and no longer the bytes that were signed.

### 2. This asks for special-category data under GDPR

Sexual activity, children, a partner, a disability, a physical problem: Article
9 data, all four. Explicit consent is required per purpose, a DPIA is on the
mandatory list for this kind of processing at scale, and the Danish DPA is the
supervisory authority. That is a real obligation and it is not satisfied by a
checkbox in an onboarding flow.

The good news is that the architecture already answers most of it. Records are
AES-GCM ciphertext under a key derived from the member's passphrase; the server
holds envelopes it cannot read. A subject access request is a JSON export the
member already has, and erasure is deleting a profile, which already works.

**Decisions:**

- Sensitive answers ride in the same encrypted envelope as everything else. No
  new mechanism, and the server's blindness is the existing guarantee rather
  than a new promise. The alternative — device-only, never synced — buys a
  stronger sentence and loses the data when a phone does, and it needs a second
  code path in `records.ts` for one field group.
- **Every intimate answer is separately deletable without touching the rest of
  the profile.** One button, one record, gone from every device on the next
  sync. This is the concession that makes the sync decision defensible.
- **No sensitive answer is ever sent to a coach that is not self-hosted.** See
  problem 3.
- The DPIA is written before Phase 3 ships, not after. It is a document, it is
  Edd's to sign, and it is listed as Task 0.3.

### 3. The coach is somebody else's computer

`coachDestination()` in `src/lib/ai-plan.ts` already returns `host: 'self' |
'external'`, and its own comment explains why that distinction was worth
building: "the wrong way to be wrong is to promise somebody their words stayed
home when they did not." Today that governs a sentence above a text box. Here it
governs whether a request is made at all.

**Decision: the companion runs at three tiers, and the tier is decided by the
host, not by the user's patience.**

| Question sensitivity | External coach | Self-hosted coach | No coach |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ordinary` (wake time, work hours, commute) | sent | sent | asked in bank order |
| `personal` (partner, children, disability, pain) | **never sent** | sent | asked in bank order |
| `intimate` (the adults-only module) | **never sent** | sent | asked in bank order |

The deterministic path is not a degraded mode. The question bank is ordered and
the fallback asks the next unanswered question, which is what a form does. What
the model buys is skipping questions that are already implied and phrasing the
follow-up; it is not load-bearing, and the feature ships useful without it.

### 4. There is no licensed imagery for the intimacy module

The catalogue's 1,322 movements come with pictures because
`yuhonas/free-exercise-db` is Unlicense and RepDB's free tier permits in-app
use. Neither covers sexual positions, no public-domain dataset of them exists
in a usable form, and the product's own rule — "nothing is fabricated to look
like an illustration that does not exist" — forbids the obvious shortcut.

**Decision: text and typographic tiles at launch, commissioned line art later
or never.** The existing CSS muscle tile is the fallback for movements with no
picture; the same component serves here. Commissioned original illustration is
a real cost (Task 0.5) and a real risk with payment processors, and the module
is useful without it.

Related and non-negotiable: **non-explicit throughout.** Anatomically plain
language, no photography, no video, no erotica. This is what keeps the account
inside a payment processor's acceptable-use policy, and the line is not one to
approach and see what happens.

## Data model

### Client records (encrypted, inside the profile)

Three additions to `COLLECTIONS` in `src/lib/records.ts`, plus their entries in
`ORDER`, `recordsFromSnapshot`, `snapshotFromRecords` and `GymSnapshot`.

```ts
/** Singleton. What a day is made of, for this person. */
interface LifeProfile {
  /** Fixed hours they do not control. Local time, 24h, "HH:MM". */
  anchors: Anchor[]
  wake?: string
  sleep?: string
  /** Free prose, verbatim, the way OnboardingInput.constraints already works. */
  notes?: string
  /** Answers to the bank, by question id. Absent means unanswered, not "no". */
  answers: Record<string, string>
  /** Which sensitivity tiers they agreed to be asked. Explicit consent, per tier. */
  consent: { personal?: string; intimate?: string }  // ISO timestamp of the yes
  updatedAt: string
}

interface Anchor {
  id: string
  label: string                    // "school run", "work", "commute"
  days: DayOfWeek[]
  start: string                    // "08:30"
  end: string
  kind: 'work' | 'care' | 'travel' | 'fixed' | 'busy'
  /** Set when it came from an imported calendar rather than from a person. */
  source?: 'ics'
}

/** One per date. Written when the day is first opened, not generated ahead. */
interface DayPlan {
  date: string                     // YYYY-MM-DD
  slots: DaySlot[]
  /** One short line from the companion, or absent. Never medical, never a nag. */
  note?: string
  generatedAt: string
}

interface DaySlot {
  start: string
  end: string
  kind: 'anchor' | 'training' | 'meal' | 'challenge' | 'culture' | 'rest' | 'intimacy'
  /** Points at what it is: an exerciseId, a recipe id, a message id, an anchor id. */
  ref?: string
  label: string
  /** The member moved or removed it. A regenerate must not undo that. */
  pinned?: boolean
}
```

`intimacyLog` is deliberately **not** a `Workout`. Putting it in `workouts`
would push it through `stats.ts`, `muscle-volume.ts`, the 1RM chart, the PR
badge and the history export, which is four surfaces that would then have to
learn to hide it.

```ts
/** Its own collection, its own row, excluded from every training aggregate. */
interface IntimacyEntry {
  id: string
  date: string
  /** Minutes. Optional — the module works with nothing but a date. */
  minutes?: number
  /** Ids from src/data/intimacy.ts, never from the exercise catalogue. */
  activityIds?: string[]
  mood?: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5
}
```

### Server

```
users.pro_until   date, empty for everybody. The only entitlement fact.
users.pro_source  text, max 16: 'stripe' | 'grant' | 'gym'

billing_events    id (Stripe event id, unique), type, owner, handled, created
                  listRule/viewRule: platform admins only. No card data, ever.
```

`billing_events` exists for one reason: Stripe delivers webhooks at least once,
sometimes twice, and a duplicate `checkout.session.completed` must not extend a
subscription by two months. The unique index on the Stripe event id is the whole
idempotency mechanism.

Nothing about the day plan reaches the server. `records.col` is free text, so
`lifeProfile`, `dayPlans` and `intimacyLog` sync as ciphertext through the
existing engine with no migration and no rule change.

## Entitlement, and what happens offline

A local-first app cannot ask a server whether somebody has paid before it will
draw a screen. `capabilities.ts` already solved the shape of this: ask, cache in
localStorage, boot on yesterday's answer.

`src/lib/entitlement.ts` copies that pattern with one addition — the cached
answer carries its own expiry and a grace window:

- `pro_until` in the future → Pro.
- Cached answer, offline, within **14 days** of the last successful check → Pro.
  Somebody who paid does not lose their planner on a plane.
- Cached answer older than the grace window → not Pro, with a line saying it
  could not be checked rather than a line implying they did not pay.

Client-side gating is UX. The enforcement that matters is server-side and lands
in exactly one place: the coach route in `shared_fetches.pb.js` already meters
and caps calls, and gains an entitlement check for the `life` prompt kind. A
member who forges a localStorage flag gets the screens and no companion, which
is the correct outcome — the screens cost us nothing and the model costs money.

**Price: EUR 15 a month, decided, tax on top.** One price and one interval.
Annual, coupons and trials are each a second number to reason about, a second
thing for the copy to state correctly and a second path through whatever charges
the card; HunterReady's own non-goals say the same. `PRICES` in `gym-plan.ts` is
B2B and stays; `PRO_PRICE` lives in `src/lib/member-plan.ts` beside a `BUILT`
set, so the copy cannot claim a feature the gate refuses. That constraint is
lifted verbatim from `gym-plan.ts` and has already paid for itself once.

At EUR 15 the coach's cost per member is what decides whether the margin holds,
and `coach_usage` has the rows to answer it. The cap today is twenty calls a day
per account.

The entitlement. Boring, blocking, and the only phase with a real security
boundary in it. Shipped on `feat/member-pro`; Stripe moved out to Phase 7.

**What was built**

```

src/lib/entitlement-hook.spec.ts                           the server predicate, tested
src/components/pro-section.tsx                             the Settings panel
```

Modified: `src/store/useSession.ts` (a `pro` field, for the same reason `role` is
there), `src/components/app-shell.tsx` (adopt from cache on unlock, then ask),
`src/routes/Settings.tsx`, `scripts/audit/run.mjs`, `.gitignore`.

**Three things this plan got wrong, found by running it**

1. **A request hook fires for a superuser's PATCH.** The guard refused the grant
   script from its own hook. The exemption is `e.hasSuperuserAuth()` and not
   membership of `platform_admins`: an account carrying the app's admin role is
   a member of this product with a role, not the operator of the server, and
   `pro-boundary.mjs` checks that one cannot grant itself Pro. The operator who
   holds the dashboard and the database file is a different matter, and refusing
   them would have protected nothing.
2. **`pro_until` has two shapes and one of them lies.** It reads back as a
   string over the API and as a `types.DateTime` inside the JSVM, where
   `String()` on it yields `[object Object]`. That parses to NaN, which reads as
   "not paid", so every paying account on the server would have been refused
   while every test written against the API's strings kept passing. Hence
   `dateText()` as a named, tested step rather than an inline coercion, and
   hence the guard comparing text rather than two DateTime objects that are
   never `===`.
3. **`billing_events` was not needed yet.** It is the idempotency ledger for a
   webhook, so it moved to Phase 7 with the webhook. A migration should carry
   what is used.

**Two things deliberately not built**

- **`pro-gate.tsx`.** Nothing sits behind Pro yet, so its shape is the first Pro
  screen's to decide. `proAllows()` is the gate; a component wrapping it can
  wait for a caller.
- **A price on screen.** `anythingBuilt()` is false, so the subscription panel
  says nothing is sold yet rather than asking for money in exchange for a
  promise. That sentence changes by itself when a feature joins `BUILT`.

**What the walk proves that a unit test cannot**

`test-pro.mjs` stops the sandbox before its last unlock. With a reachable server
the cache is never consulted, because a page load probes `/me` anyway, so the
check would pass whether or not `adoptEntitlement` existed. Offline is the only
place the cache is load-bearing, and it is exactly where a paying member sits
when it matters.

Also recorded, because it cost an hour: the agent preview pane anchors its
working directory to the repository root and cannot serve a worktree. Walks
against a branch need `PORT=3016 pnpm exec vite preview` from the worktree,
which `vite.config.ts` already supports and says so in a comment.

**Files:**

- Create `src/components/consent-step.tsx` — the per-tier consent, written in

- Modify `deploy/pocketbase/pb_hooks/shared_fetches.pb.js` — the `life` prompt

**Steps:**

- [ ] **3.1** The bank, in both languages, ordered from ordinary to intimate. A
      question's sensitivity is a property of the question, written once, and
      every gate reads it — there is no second list to keep in step.
- [ ] **3.2** `nextQuestion` deterministic and tested. This is the whole feature
      when there is no coach, so it is the path with the most tests, not the
      fewest.
- [ ] **3.3** The tier gate, in `life-coach.ts`, as its own exported function
      with its own tests — the same reasoning that put `coachHostFor` in a file
      of its own. **`personal` and `intimate` answers are filtered out of the
      payload when `host !== 'self'`, at the point of building the request,**
      not at the point of deciding whether to send. Filtering where the bytes
      are assembled is what survives somebody later adding a field.
- [ ] **3.4** Consent per tier, timestamped in the record, withdrawable. Refusing
      a tier is a complete answer: the planner works, it just knows less.
- [ ] **3.5** Delete: one button, removes every `personal` and `intimate` answer
      plus the whole intimacy log, in one action, and the tombstones travel on
      the next sync.

**Gate:** lint, test, build, plus a test that proves a sensitive answer cannot
reach an external host. That test is the reason this phase exists in this shape.

## Phase 4 — Calendars

No OAuth. Every calendar exports `.ics` and every calendar subscribes to a URL.

**Files:**

- Create `src/lib/ics.ts` — `parseIcs(text)` and `toIcs(day)`. The parsing
  subset: `VEVENT`, `DTSTART`, `DTEND`, `SUMMARY`, `RRULE` weekly only, line
  unfolding, `TZID` passed through as-is. Anything else is skipped rather than
  guessed at, which is the house rule for malformed input everywhere else.
- Create `src/lib/ics.spec.ts` — real exports from Google, Apple and Outlook as
  fixtures. All three fold lines differently and one of them will be wrong.
- Create `src/components/calendar-import.tsx` — file picker, a preview of what
  was found, and a checkbox per event before anything becomes an anchor.
- Modify `src/routes/Day.tsx` — import and export.

**Steps:**

- [ ] **4.1** Parse to busy blocks. **Titles are dropped on import by default**
      — the planner needs to know somebody is busy at ten, not that it is a
      divorce hearing. Keeping titles is a checkbox and it is off.
- [ ] **4.2** Export a day or a week as `.ics`, downloaded locally. Nothing is
      uploaded and no server is involved.
- [ ] **4.3** *(later, own decision)* A `webcal` feed per profile so the plan
      appears in their calendar and refreshes. This needs the server to hold
      **plaintext** slot titles behind an unguessable URL, which is a real
      reversal of the product's promise for the sake of convenience. If it is
      built: titles are generic ("Training", "Meal"), the intimacy module never
      appears in it at all, and the URL is revocable.
- [ ] **4.4** Two-way Google/Microsoft sync is deliberately not in this plan. It
      means our server holding a token that can read somebody's whole calendar,
      forever, and that is a different product with a different privacy story.

## Phase 6 — Intimate activity

Last, optional, and behind three gates at once: Pro, an 18+ affirmation, and an
explicit opt-in that is off by default. Blocked on Task 0.2 and 0.5.

**Files:**

- Create `src/data/intimacy.ts` — its own type `IntimacyActivity`, its own file,
  its own module. **Not** merged into `exercises-generated.ts`, which is
  rebuilt by `scripts/import-exercises.mjs` and would drop it on the next run —
  and which feeds `allowedExerciseIds()`, meaning a merged row could end up in a
  generated training programme. The wger dataset is already kept separate for a
  parallel reason and the precedent is exactly right.
- Create `src/lib/intimacy.ts` — the log, its energy estimate, and the exclusion
  helpers.
- Create `src/routes/Intimacy.tsx`, `src/components/intimacy-log.tsx`.
- Create `src/lib/intimacy.spec.ts` — and the exclusion tests belong here:
  an entry must not appear in `stats.ts`, `muscle-volume.ts`, the PR check, the
  history export, any gym reach count, or any notification body.
- Modify `src/lib/records.ts` and `src/store/useGym.ts` for `intimacyLog`.

**Steps:**

- [ ] **6.1** Content: plain anatomical language, effort described in
      qualitative terms and in MET-equivalents **with the citation in the data
      file**. The literature puts partnered sexual activity in the light-to-
      moderate range in short bouts; the measured energy cost is far below the
      figure that circulates in magazines. The product refuses to lie about
      training timelines and it will not start lying here.
- [ ] **6.2** Accessibility and physical limitation are the actual product here,
      not an afterthought: the `personal` tier already asked about pain,
      mobility and disability, and the module's one genuinely useful act is
      filtering for what somebody's body allows. That is worth building well.
- [ ] **6.3** No streaks, no goals, no "you are behind this week". A frequency
      target on somebody's sex life is a way to make them feel worse, and the
      product's voice is factual, never motivational — a commitment that has
      never mattered more than it does on this screen.
- [ ] **6.4** Never in a notification body, never in the inbox, never in a gym
      panel, never in the `webcal` feed, and excluded from the JSON export
      unless the member ticks a box on the way out.
- [ ] **6.5** A hide switch that removes the module from the nav entirely — the
      phone gets handed to other people.

## Phase 7 — Stripe, last

Last because payment is the only part of this that can be replaced by a person
running a script, and because none of it can be verified until Task 0.2 answers.
The entitlement it writes into already exists, is guarded, and is proved from
both sides, so this phase adds one route, one receipt endpoint and one ledger.

**Files:**

- Create `deploy/pocketbase/pb_migrations/1758800000_billing_events.js` —
  the ledger, plus `users.stripe_customer`. That last field is why the ledger
  cannot wait: `invoice.paid` carries a customer and no `client_reference_id`,
  so the account a renewal belongs to is only findable through it.
- Create `deploy/pocketbase/pb_hooks/utils/stripe_sig.js` — header parsing,
  timestamp tolerance, and the comparison. Pure, with the HMAC injected, so
  `src/lib/stripe-sig.spec.ts` can test it the way `entitlement-hook.spec.ts`
  tests its own file: the shipped bytes, evaluated, not a copy.
- Create `deploy/pocketbase/pb_hooks/billing.pb.js` — `POST
  /api/enforma/checkout` (authed, hosted Checkout Session, `automatic_tax` on,
  `client_reference_id` the user id) and `POST /api/enforma/stripe-webhook`.
- Modify `shared_fetches.pb.js` — capabilities gains `billing`, true only when
  a key and a price id are both set.
- Modify `src/lib/capabilities.ts`, `src/components/pro-section.tsx` — the
  button, behind `caps.billing && anythingBuilt()`.
- Modify `scripts/audit/pro-boundary.mjs` — the webhook half of the boundary.
- Modify `.env.example` — `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`, `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`,
  `STRIPE_PRICE_MONTHLY`.

**Steps:**

- [ ] **7.1** Signature first, on the raw body, before anything is parsed.
- [ ] **7.2** Insert `(livemode, event_id)` and abort on the unique constraint
      **before** doing any work. Doing it after is how a redelivery buys a
      second month.
- [ ] **7.3** Derive expected livemode from the key prefix (`sk_live_` against
      `sk_test_`) rather than from another environment variable, and refuse a
      mismatch. Do not pin `api_version`: the fields read here have been stable
      for years and a pinned version breaks payments silently on a Stripe-side
      bump.
- [ ] **7.4** `checkout.session.completed` links `stripe_customer` and sets
      `pro_source`. `invoice.paid` sets `pro_until` from
      `lines.data[0].period.end`. `customer.subscription.deleted` does nothing,
      because the date expires by itself. If a period end cannot be read, the
      ledger row stays `handled: false` and a line is logged: a fallback that
      invents a date is the kind of thing that goes wrong quietly.
- [ ] **7.5** Cancellation is a link to Stripe's own billing portal. We do not
      build a cancel button; Stripe has one and it is legally theirs to get
      right.
- [ ] **7.6** No card field anywhere in this codebase, in any phase. The Stripe
      customer id on the user row is the entire footprint.
- [ ] **7.7** Extend `pro-boundary.mjs`: an unsigned body moves nothing, a
      replayed event grants nothing twice, a livemode mismatch is refused, and a
      member cannot read `billing_events`.
- [ ] **7.8** Verify with Stripe's CLI event forwarder against the local
      PocketBase. The Hetzner box already has `stripe-listen-*.log` files for
      two other products, so a third fits an existing pattern.
